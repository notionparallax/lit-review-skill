# Stage 1: Scope and budget

**Purpose.** Agree how big this review is before anything else, so that every later stage is sized to fit. People tend to underestimate how long reading and checking take, and overestimate how many words they need. A review that is too big for the time available ends half-checked, which is worse than a smaller review that is fully checked.

**Output.** `01_scope.md`, with the size tier and the numbers below filled in.

## What to ask

Ask these, one or two at a time, in conversation, skipping or confirming anything the person has already told you (purpose and reader often arrive together in their first message). Start with the germ of the idea itself (one or two sentences in their words); stage 2 sharpens it, but you need it now to judge size.

1. **What is the idea?** In their words. Don't refine it yet.
2. **What is it for?** The decision or piece of work it will feed: a design brief, a client conversation, a research proposal, a talk, a practice position, a paper. This sets the reader and the standard of evidence.
3. **Who will read it?** A colleague, a client, a project team, a board, an academic partner. Ask what that reader already knows.
4. **How much of your own time can you give it?** In hours or days, over what period. Be explicit that this is *their* time (reading key papers, making decisions, commenting on drafts, fetching PDFs), not elapsed time.
5. **How long should the finished review be?** In words, or pages (≈500 words a page). If they don't know, recommend from the tier table.
6. **Is there a deadline?**
7. **Language and conventions.** Spelling (default Australian English), reference style (default APA 7). Only ask if there's a reason to think they want something else.

## Size tiers

Use this table to recommend a tier from their answers, and to show them what their time buys. The figures are rough guides from experience, not rules; adjust them openly when the topic is unusually thin or dense.

| | Scan | Briefing | Full review |
|---|---|---|---|
| Person's time | 3 to 8 hours | 2 to 5 days | 3 weeks or more |
| Elapsed time | 1 to 3 days | 1 to 4 weeks | 1 to 3 months |
| Length | 1,500 to 3,000 words | 4,000 to 8,000 words | 8,000 to 15,000 words |
| Sources cited | 15 to 35 | 40 to 100 | 100 to 300 |
| Sections or themes | 2 to 4 | 4 to 7 | 6 to 10 |
| Search | 3 to 6 logged queries, plus seed papers | A planned set of strings, one round of reference chasing | Full plan with a test of the strings against seed papers |
| Person reads | 3 to 5 key papers (skim) | 8 to 15 key papers | 20 or more |
| Comment rounds | 1 | 1 or 2, plus an outside critique | 2 or more, plus outside critique |

**How the person's time is spent** (use this to sanity-check their budget):

- Reading: ≈1 hour to read a paper properly, ≈15 minutes to skim one (abstract, introduction, figures, conclusion).
- Decisions at stage gates: ≈30 to 60 minutes per stage at briefing tier.
- Fetching PDFs through their library: ≈5 minutes each.
- Commenting on a draft: ≈1 hour per 2,000 words per round.

If their time and their word count disagree (for example 4 hours and 10,000 words), say so plainly and offer the two honest options: fewer words, or more time. A long review on little time can only be produced by skipping checks, and the skill does not do that.

**Word density.** A review typically cites one source per 50 to 100 words. Many more words per source suggests padding or an argument running ahead of its evidence; many fewer suggests a list of studies rather than an argument.

## What to write in `01_scope.md`

Fill in the template: the idea in their words, purpose, reader, time budget, word target, deadline, tier, conventions, and a short "what this tier means for us" paragraph that says, concretely, how many sources, which stages are light, and how many papers they will read themselves. Date the decisions.

## Gate

The person agrees the tier and the numbers. Record any later change to them in `01_scope.md` with the date and the reason, since a scope that grows silently is how a scan becomes an unfinished full review.
