# Stage 6: Argument and outline

**Purpose.** Agree what the review argues before writing any paragraphs. A review is an argument about the state of knowledge, not a tour of studies, and its structure follows from that argument. Restructuring a drafted review costs far more than agreeing a spine up front.

**Output.** `06_outline.md`.

## 1. The spine

Draft the review's argument in **three short paragraphs**, from the theme summaries in `05_sources.md`:

1. **Why the question is worth asking now**: the decisions or practice that rest on it, and what is wrong or uncertain in how it is usually answered.
2. **What is known**: the main findings that hold across studies, with how strong the evidence is.
3. **What is not known**: the gaps, contradictions or weaknesses that remain, and what kind of evidence would resolve them.

Offer it as a draft for the person to rewrite. This is the most important judgement in the review and it is theirs. If their hypotheses from stage 2 turned out wrong, the spine should say so plainly; point it out if their draft leans on a hunch the sources didn't support.

## 2. The outline

From the agreed spine, set out the sections:

- Numbered sections, each with a one-line purpose (what it establishes for the argument) and a word budget. Budgets should add up to the stage 1 target; aim to finish within ±15% of each.
- Under each section, the sources it will draw on (by key) and the main claims it will make, each claim tagged with its supporting keys.
- An introduction (purpose, question, scope note from stage 2, a short method paragraph saying how sources were found and checked) and a conclusion (what is known, what is not, implications for the person's purpose).
- A short **methods landscape** for each theme (Part B of [methods.md](methods.md)): which designs dominate and what that means for how far the findings can be trusted.
- A gap list (briefing and full tiers): each gap says why the evidence is insufficient and what data or method would address it. Talk through the gaps that bear on the person's purpose using Part C of [methods.md](methods.md): what study would close each one, and what is feasible for them.

**Check the outline against the sources.** Flag any planned claim with no supporting key, and any key source that appears nowhere. Flag sections resting on one study or only on GREY sources.

## By tier

- **Scan.** Spine and outline together on one page; two to four sections; no gap list (a closing paragraph on gaps instead).
- **Briefing and full.** As above.

## Gate

The person agrees the spine and outline. Record the agreed spine with the date. Update `STATUS.md` and commit.
