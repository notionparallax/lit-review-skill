# Verification and interest

A review is only as strong as the check behind each claim. These rules apply from the first source note to the last edit.

## Status of each source

Record one in `05_sources.md` for every source, when it is first noted. The statuses are working notes; they never appear in the rendered reference list.

- **VERIFIED.** The record or page was retrieved in this project and the claim checked against it. Say at what level: `VERIFIED (full text)` or `VERIFIED (abstract)`. Cite freely, within what that level supports.
- **PARTIAL.** Known only indirectly: a search snippet, a catalogue record, a secondary summary, or an abstract when the claim goes beyond it. Keep the claim modest, and if it carries weight, say in the text how it is known. List it in `08_checks.md` for follow-up.
- **GREY.** Not peer reviewed: reports from government, industry bodies, consultancies, vendors, law firms; web pages; press. Combine with a verification level (`GREY; VERIFIED (full report)`). Always add an interest note (below), and state it in the text at first citation.
- **UNVERIFIED.** Couldn't be found or opened, or the record is garbled. Do not cite it. Note it as dropped, with the reason, so it isn't rediscovered and used later.

## Rules

- **Never fill a gap from memory.** Your training data contains plausible but wrong figures, authors, years and titles, and press coverage often misquotes papers. If you can't retrieve it, it isn't known in this project.
- **Use the direction, not the number,** when only a secondary account of a figure is available, or cut the sentence.
- **Mark your own inferences** as inferences in the text ('this suggests', with what it rests on), and list them in `08_checks.md`.
- **When sources disagree** on a fact, don't choose silently. State the disagreement, or use the primary source and record the discrepancy in the notes.
- **Time-sensitive claims** (law, pending policy, market figures) carry the 'as at' date from `02_question.md`.
- **Don't cite or draw on the person's own unpublished work** unless they say to.

## Interest

For each source, ask: does an author, funder or data owner stand to gain from the finding? Record the type and the reason in `05_sources.md`. The aim is not to exclude interested sources, which are often the only data, but to let the reader weigh them, and to set them beside independent sources on the same question where those exist.

| Type | Meaning | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| independent | No stake that bears on the topic | Normal |
| commercial | An author, funder or data owner sells into the market the study is about (including a corporate research lab evaluating its own product) | Flag in text; look for independent corroboration |
| data vendor | The data come from a firm that sells into the market, even if no author works there | Flag in text; note methods disclosed or not |
| own instrument | The authors judge their own survey or tool against rivals | Flag |
| own organisation | Authors study their own institution, with no product at stake | Flag lightly; weight as near-independent |
| advocacy | A union, industry association, lobby or campaign group | Flag; treat as a statement of position, not evidence, unless it publishes data and method |
| government | An agency reporting on its own performance or policy | Flag; an agency testing its own statistics' accuracy is not interested |

Being a listed author counts, whatever the person's role. Check affiliations, funding statements, data sources and competing-interest declarations; don't rely on the journal's reputation.

**Interest note format** (in `05_sources.md`, and briefly in the text at first citation): who they are, what they sell or stand to gain, the likely direction of any bias, and whether the method is disclosed. Example: "Vendor of desk-booking software; benefits from findings that offices are under-used; sample and method not published."
